- # Lab Plastid Genome Visualization — Answers

## 1. Scientific name and accession number

The scientific name of the chosen plant is *Paeonia suffruticosa*, and its plastid genome accession number is NC_037879.1.

## 2. Total plastid genome length

The total length of the *Paeonia suffruticosa* chloroplast genome is 153,119 bp.

## 3. LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions

The map shows four major structural regions. The LSC (Large Single-Copy) is the largest region and occupies most of the upper portion of the circular map. The SSC (Small Single-Copy) is the smaller region located at the lower portion of the map. IRa (Inverted Repeat A) is located between the SSC and LSC on the lower-right side, while IRb (Inverted Repeat B) is located between the SSC and LSC on the lower-left side. Together, these regions form the LSC–IRb–SSC–IRa organization of the circular plastid genome.

## 4. Three genes in the LSC region

Three examples of genes located in the LSC region are psaA, psbA, and rbcL.

## 5. Gene in the SSC region

One example of a gene located in the SSC region is ndhF. Other genes visible in or associated with the SSC include ndhA, ndhD, ndhG, ndhI, rpl32, rps15, and ycf1.

## 6. Gene in an inverted repeat

An example of a gene located within an inverted repeat region is rrn16, a ribosomal RNA gene. Yes, genes located within the IR regions can appear more than once because IRa and IRb contain duplicated copies of the same sequence.

## 7. Photosynthesis-related gene

The rbcL gene is a photosynthesis-related gene visible on the map. It encodes the large subunit of Rubisco, an enzyme involved in carbon fixation during photosynthesis.

## 8. Direction of transcription

The arrows show the direction in which genes are transcribed. Genes pointing in opposite directions are transcribed from opposite DNA strands. Therefore, plastid genes have different transcriptional orientations rather than all being transcribed in the same direction.

## 9. GC content

The GC content does not appear completely uniform across the plastid genome. The inner GC-content graph shows fluctuations at different positions around the circular genome, indicating regional variation in GC content. The overall GC content measured previously was 38.38%.

## 10. Usefulness of a graphical genome map

A graphical plastid genome map makes the organization of the genome much easier to understand visually. It allows the locations of the LSC, SSC, and IR regions, gene positions, gene orientations, duplicated IR genes, and GC-content patterns to be seen at the same time. A raw DNA sequence or long annotation list is much harder to interpret as a whole.
